Polsat Box: how CPS spent hundreds of millions building a streaming platform nobody wants to pay for

With 1.2 million subscribers against Netflix's estimated 4.8 million Polish accounts, Polsat Box is the most expensive lesson in Central European streaming history. The group has now pivoted to bundling it with mobile contracts — an admission that the standalone product cannot compete on its own merits.

Author: Technology & Streaming deskDate: April 27, 2026Reading time: 5 min

When Cyfrowy Polsat CPS rebranded its streaming offer as Polsat Box in 2022 and committed to a multi-year content investment strategy, the pitch to investors was credible: a broadcaster with deep local content archives, exclusive Polish sports rights, and an existing satellite subscriber base had natural advantages over global platforms entering an unfamiliar market. Three years later, the scorecard is uncomfortable. Polsat Box ended 2025 with approximately 1.2 million subscribers. Netflix NFLX holds an estimated 4.8 million Polish accounts. Max (HBO) and Disney+ DIS combined account for a further estimated 3 million.

The subscriber gap would be less damning if Polsat Box were growing. It is not, meaningfully. The group's own reporting buries subscriber counts in footnotes and declines to provide year-on-year comparisons in its investor presentations — a disclosure choice that speaks for itself. Analysts at Wood & Company estimate that Polsat Box's average revenue per user (ARPU) is materially below both Netflix and Max, partly because a significant share of its subscriber base accessed the service through Plus mobile bundles rather than standalone sign-ups.

Bundling your streaming service with a mobile contract is not a growth strategy. It is a rescue operation dressed up as a partnership.

The pivot to bundling Polsat Box with Plus mobile contracts — announced in late 2024 and aggressively pushed in 2025 — is an implicit admission that the platform cannot attract subscribers on the strength of its content alone. The move has stabilised headline subscriber numbers while depressing ARPU and making meaningful subscriber quality assessment impossible. For CPS investors on the WSE, the streaming division remains a black box: hundreds of millions in content spend, opaque subscriber disclosure, and no credible pathway to profitability that management has been willing to put on paper.
